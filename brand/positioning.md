# Brand Positioning

> Built from `context.md` and `audience.md`. Competitor descriptions come from general knowledge and should be verified before public use. Lisette is pre-launch, so proof points are **planned capabilities**, not live evidence.

## 01 — Category Definition

- **Stated category**: A literary hub for writers, readers and publishers.
- **Actual category**: What people compare Lisette against depends on who they are.
  - Writers compare it to submission trackers (Submittable, Duotrope, Query Tracker), spreadsheets and email.
  - Readers compare it to Goodreads, StoryGraph and LibraryThing.
  - Writers who want an audience compare it to social platforms and Wattpad.
- **Opportunity category**: *Literary discovery and opportunity*, connecting writers, readers and publishers through submissions, shared writing, books and literary experiences. This is intended positioning, not a verified claim of competitive uniqueness.

**Recommendation:** Centre the connection between writers, readers and publishers. Each is equally important: writers find opportunities and readers, readers discover voices and literary experiences, and publishers discover authors and build connections with their community. Recruitment can start with debut writers without making other participants secondary.

## 02 — Competitive Landscape Mapping

**Axis 1:** Industry process ← → Reader community
**Axis 2:** Tools for finished books ← → Tools for books in the making

| Competitor | Where it sits | Territory it owns |
|---|---|---|
| Goodreads / StoryGraph | Reader community, finished books | Tracking and rating books people have read |
| LibraryThing | Reader community, finished books | Cataloguing and collecting |
| Submittable / Duotrope / Query Tracker | Industry process, books in the making | Managing and tracking submissions |
| Wattpad | Reader community, books in the making | Serial reading and writing for a mass audience |
| Twitter/X, Bluesky | Neutral social space | Where writers talk, not tied to any book process |
| Email and spreadsheets | Industry process | The status quo for submissions |

**Where Lisette sits:** in the middle-to-upper area between *industry process* and *reader community*, centred on *books in the making*, with writers, readers and publishers sharing the same space.

**Intended territory:** connect the industry side (publishers receiving manuscripts) with the reader side (people who follow and back authors). Lisette's focus is the bridge between the two; competitive uniqueness remains unverified.

## 03 — Positioning Territory

**The Space:** A shared literary space where writers, readers and publishers discover each other, new stories and opportunities.

**The Audience Owned:** Three equally important participants: writers seeking publishing opportunities and readers; readers discovering voices, books and literary experiences; and publishers discovering authors, reviewing submissions and connecting with their community. Debut writers are the first recruitment group, not a higher-ranking brand audience.

**The Competitive Moat:**
- *Connection across three groups.* The intended advantage is bringing writers, readers and publishers into one literary community. Competitor comparisons remain to be verified. Once Lisette has real writers, readers and publishers on one platform, each side makes the other more valuable, and that network is hard to copy.
- *Trust.* Encrypted manuscripts, clear access permissions and device authentication make Lisette the safe place for unpublished work, and trust is slow to build.

These moats only hold if the platform actually reaches critical mass. Today they are plans.

## 04 — Positioning Statement

**Core:**
*"Lisette connects writers, readers and publishers through manuscript submissions, shared excerpts, personal libraries and literary discoveries. It creates opportunities for connection without guaranteeing publication."*

**Strategic version (internal):**
*"For writers, readers and publishers, Lisette brings publishing opportunities and literary discovery into one community. Debut writers are the initial recruitment group; all three groups are equally central."*

## 05 — Positioning Proof Points

These are planned capabilities; final first-release scope still needs planning. Do not claim any publicly until they work.

- **Proof 1: Submit once, track everything.** One manuscript package goes to several publishers, with the confirmed states: Submitted, In review, On hold, Selected by publisher, Rejected and Withdrawn. Evaluations accompany decisions rather than forming a separate status.
- **Proof 2: Your work stays yours.** Full manuscripts are encrypted and visible only to the publishers the author selects, with a submitter declaration, device-authenticated upload confirmation and a record of the uploaded version and time. Device authentication allows biometrics or the phone PIN/password; it does not verify authorship.
- **Proof 3: Readers can back authors.** Follow, read author-selected excerpts early as a beta-reader, after any publisher approvals required by accepted submission restrictions, review, share, and get notified when authors publish.
- **Proof 4: One home for the library.** Scan ISBNs with the camera to build a personal library, and see what the authors you follow are reading.

## 06 — What This Brand Refuses to Be

- Lisette is not for writers who want to sell books directly. It is a route to publishers and readers, not a store.
- There is no financial layer at this stage. Supporting authors means following, reading, reviewing, sharing and recommending; future financial features are undecided.
- Lisette does not try to be a general-purpose social network. The feed exists for the literary community.
- Lisette is not just a ratings app. A reader who only wants a quick star rating is better served by Goodreads.

## 07 — Positioning in One Sentence

Lisette connects writers, readers and publishers so new stories can find opportunities and people who want to read them.

## Red Flags

- **Breadth risk.** Three pillars (manuscript hub, writers' network, library) can read like "an app for everything". The centre is literary discovery and opportunity across writers, readers and publishers. Each feature should support that purpose; it does not have to serve all three groups on every screen.
- **Proof points aren't proven.** Everything in section 05 is planned. For the competition demo, show it working. Don't claim outcomes (such as "better feedback") until real data supports them.
- **Community cold start.** The value depends on writers, readers and publishers all being present. The demo's simulated publishers and labelled test content are fine for the competition, but any real launch needs a plan to bring all three groups together.
- **Balance at brand level; focus by context.** The homepage and overall brand story represent writers, readers and publishers equally. Individual screens, notifications and targeted campaigns address the relevant audience, including publishers, without having to mention the others.
- **"Hub" is generic.** It describes the shape of the product, not the benefit. Keep it as a descriptor, not the tagline.
- **Table-stakes risk.** "Social network" and "library" are table stakes. The real differentiators are the connection between publisher submissions and a reader community. The reading heatmap is a deferred possibility, not a core differentiator.
