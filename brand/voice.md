# Brand Voice Guide

> Built from `context.md`, `audience.md` and `positioning.md`. Romanian and English are both launch languages, chosen by each person (see `brand.yaml` and `context.md`). Existing English examples are reference copy; adapt them naturally into Romanian with the same voice. Approved Romanian tagline: "Unde editurile își găsesc autorii, iar cărțile, cititorii." Keep the existing English tagline; the two expressions need not be literal translations. The personality traits warm, curious, protective, supportive and literary are approved (2026-10-17). The calm, thoughtful, conversational tone and occasional gentle bookish humour are also approved. No jokes in errors, rejection or privacy messages. Copy examples remain illustrative: use them only when their product claims match confirmed functionality and state.

## 01 — Voice Overview

**Approved personality:** warm, curious, protective, supportive and literary.

Lisette speaks like a well-read friend who loves books and wants good writing to find its readers. It is warm without being gushing, and thoughtful without being stuffy. It treats writers as professionals who deserve clarity and respect, and readers as allies who help books grow. It is plain-spoken with a light literary touch, and it never hypes. Lisette gives writers, readers and publishers equal importance across the brand. Individual screens and messages speak directly to the relevant audience: writers, readers, or publishers.

**Voice Essence:** *Lisette sounds like a well-read friend who cares about writers, readers and publishers, warm, thoughtful, and quietly protective.*

## 02 — Tone Dimensions

**Formality: Casual ←——●—→ Formal** (casual, but not sloppy)
- Conversational and clear, with correct grammar. No slang, no corporate stiffness.
- *"Your manuscript is on its way to three publishers."*

**Energy: Calm ←—●——→ Energetic**
- Steady and encouraging. Excitement shows in small moments, like a first submission, never in exclamation-heavy hype.
- *"Your opening pages are ready to share."*

**Humor: Serious ←—●——→ Witty** (light and gentle)
- A little dry, bookish warmth is fine. Never at the writer's expense, and never during anything stressful like rejection or privacy.
- *"Your to-read pile called. It wants company."*

**Expertise: Accessible ←●———→ Authoritative**
- Knowledgeable about books and publishing, but explained simply. Publishing jargon is defined, not assumed.
- *"A synopsis is a one-page summary of your book. Most publishers ask for one."*

**Warmth: Distant ←———●→ Intimate**
- Warm and personal, but with respect for privacy. Close like a good friend, not like a salesperson.
- *"You're in good company. Here's what other writers did next."*

## 03 — Voice Qualities

**Warm**
Lisette is kind and encouraging, especially to nervous debut writers. It celebrates meaningful completed actions without gushing: finishing a book, sending a first submission, sharing an excerpt or completing a beta-read. Personal reading totals and history are welcome as records, not scores. Never use streaks, points, rankings, countdowns or guilt about inactivity. Celebrate completed actions; never punish absence or create pressure to return.
- **Do:** "Your manuscript is submitted. That took courage."
- **Don't:** "Congratulations!!! You're one step closer to superstardom! 🎉"

**Thoughtful**
Lisette is literary and considered. It picks words with care and respects the craft of writing.
- **Do:** "Your submission is in review. You can see its progress here."
- **Don't:** "Your engagement metrics are looking great!"

**Protective**
Lisette treats unpublished work as something precious. It is clear about who can see what and never plays down privacy.
- **Do:** "Only the publishers you choose can open your full manuscript. You choose which excerpts to share. If an accepted submission policy requires publisher approval, we'll ask for it before sharing."
- **Don't:** "We take your privacy seriously." (empty without specifics)

**Supportive of all three groups**
Lisette represents writers, readers and publishers equally in brand-level communication. Audience-specific copy focuses on the person and task at hand. Readers are allies who help authors get found, and writers are people worth backing.
- **Do:** "Follow the authors you love and read their newest pages first."
- **Don't:** "Boost your favorite creators!" (creator-economy language, and it hints at money)

**Honest**
Lisette never overpromises. It doesn't promise publishing deals, and it says plainly when something is simulated or still being built.
- **Do:** "Publishers decide what they publish. We make sure your submission reaches them clearly."
- **Don't:** "Get published faster."

**Clear**
Lisette uses plain words and short steps. Anxious people don't need to decode anything.
- **Do:** "Your submission is in review. You can see where it stands."
- **Don't:** "Leverage our streamlined submission pipeline."

## 04 — Vocabulary

**Words we use:**
writer, reader, author, publisher, manuscript, draft, chapter, excerpt, shelf, library, follow, read, share, review, recommend, beta-reader, submission, opening pages, quiet, careful, together, found

**Words we avoid:**
content creator, creators, monetize, boost, hack, game-changing, revolutionary, world-class, disrupt, leverage, seamless, ecosystem, engagement (on its own), AI-powered (unless true and useful), supercharge, viral, grind, hustle

**Brand-specific language:**
- In product copy, address people by their relevant role: **writers**, **readers**, **publishers** or **editors**, rather than generic "users" or "creators". Technical documentation may use "user".
- Use **shelf** for a reader's saved books, and **library** for the whole collection.
- Use **submission** for a manuscript sent to a publisher.
- Location-based surprises are optional literary discoveries. Plain terms such as "discover" or literal "unlock" may describe access; avoid using them as exaggerated promises.
- Use Romanian diacritics in Romanian copy. English vocabulary examples are references, not a requirement to use English terms in Romanian.
- Describe supporting authors as **following, reading early, reviewing, sharing, recommending**. Never imply paying authors or being paid.
- The reading heatmap is a deferred possibility, not a promised feature. If discussed in planning, call its signals **reading patterns**, not proof of attention or writing quality.
- The app is **Lisette**, never "the Lisette app" in headlines or "LISETTE" in caps.

## 05 — Writing Style Rules

- **Sentence length:** Short to medium. Mix in a longer sentence when something deserves a pause. One idea per sentence in UI copy.
- **Punctuation:** Periods and commas first. Em-dashes sparingly, at most one per paragraph. Exclamation marks only for real moments (a first submission, a finished book), at most one per screen. Ellipses are banned.
- **Capitalization:** Sentence case for headlines, buttons and labels. Capitalize proper nouns (Lisette). Book titles in italics or quotes, not all caps.
- **Numbers:** Spell out one to nine in prose. Use numerals for 10 and above, and always for stats, counts and dates.
- **Contractions (English):** Use them. "You're", "we'll", "it's".
- **Confirmed-state reassurance:** State only what the app can confirm about saving, uploading and sending. If a saved copy is confirmed, say it is still available. If upload or submission status is unknown, say so and direct the person to check their manuscripts or submission status before retrying. Never claim a manuscript is safe or promise a retry time without evidence.
- **Active vs passive:** Active by default. Passive only when the actor doesn't matter ("Your manuscript was received").
- **Humor:** Allowed and gentle. Bookish, warm, a little dry. Never in error states, rejection or privacy messages, and never at someone's expense.
- **Emoji:** Rare. A single book or heart is fine in social posts. None in UI copy about privacy, rejection or errors.

## 06 — Channel Adaptations

| Channel | Tone Adjustment | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Website homepage | Warm and clear, with a literary touch. Represents writers, readers and publishers across the page; use the approved localized tagline. | "Unde editurile își găsesc autorii, iar cărțile, cititorii." |
| Social media | A bit more playful and conversational. Celebrates books and small wins. | "Chapter one is out. Who's reading?" |
| Email | Personal and brief. One idea and one action. | "Your submission to Hearth Press was received." |
| Support | Calm, patient, specific. No jargon, no blame. | "Sorry about that. Here's how to fix it." |
| Error messages / UI copy | Plain and reassuring. Say what happened and what to do next. | "We couldn't scan that barcode. Try again, or add the book by hand." |
| Privacy / consent screens | Serious and exact. No jokes. Spell out who sees what. | "Only the publishers you choose can read this full manuscript. Readers see only excerpts you choose to share after any required publisher approvals." |
| Publisher-facing | A bit more formal and efficient. Respectful of their time. | "Submissions arrive in one format, matched to your guidelines." |

## 07 — Before and After Examples

**Before (off-brand):**
> Supercharge your writing career with our AI-powered, game-changing submission platform!

**After (on-brand):**
> Send your manuscript once. See where it stands with every publisher.

**Why it works:** It replaces hype with a concrete benefit the writer can picture.

---

**Before:**
> We value your privacy and take security seriously.

**After:**
> Your manuscript is encrypted. Only the publishers you choose can open it.

**Why it works:** It states the actual protection instead of an empty promise.

---

**Before:**
> Support your favorite creators and help them go viral!

**After:**
> Follow the authors you love, read their new pages early, and tell others about them.

**Why it works:** It describes real ways to support authors, in book language, with no money or virality implied.

---

**Before:**
> Error 500: submission failed. Please try again later.

**After:**
When sending failed and a saved copy is confirmed:
> Your submission didn't send. Your saved manuscript is still available. Try sending it again.

When upload status is unknown:
> We couldn't confirm the upload. Check your manuscripts before trying again.

**Why it works:** It distinguishes confirmed failure from unknown status, reassures only when a saved copy is confirmed, and gives a next step.

---

**Before:**
> Boost engagement with your latest content!

**After:**
> Share your opening pages with readers.

**Why it works:** It describes a concrete action in familiar book language. Sharing remains subject to the confirmed sharing policy.

## 08 — Things We Never Say

- Hollow hype: "game-changing", "revolutionary", "world-class", "next-level".
- Promises about outcomes: "get published", "land a deal", "go viral", "become a bestseller".
- Anything that implies paying or being paid, such as "monetize your writing" or "earn from your fans" (no financial layer for now).
- Creator-economy language: "content creators", "grind", "hustle", "build your personal brand".
- Empty privacy claims ("we take your privacy seriously") without saying what is protected.
- Surveillance language about readers ("monitor", "spy on reading") or claims that reading patterns prove attention or writing quality. The reading heatmap is deferred; do not promise it in public copy.
- False urgency: "Hurry!", "Don't miss out!", "Last chance!".
- Guilt or pressure: "Your readers are waiting", "You haven't written in 3 days".
- Condescension about writers' work or about rejection: "Don't give up!" as a cliché, or blaming the writer.
- Brand-level communication that consistently sidelines writers, readers or publishers. Represent all three groups equally across the overall brand; individual screens, notifications and campaigns may address their relevant audience.
