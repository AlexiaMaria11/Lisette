# Target Audience

> **Status: hypotheses, not research.** No customer interviews, reviews or surveys exist yet. Personas, quotes, and language below are working assumptions built from `context.md` and the original concept. The "quotes" are illustrative, not real customer words. Validate with 5–10 conversations per group before using them in public copy.
> **Confirmed launch scope:** Romania is the first market, with Romanian and English interfaces from launch and international expansion intended later. The existing fictional personas remain hypotheses to validate with Romanian writers, readers and publishers.

## 01 — Audience Overview

Lisette serves three equally important groups around one thing: getting books from a writer's draft to a reader's shelf. The **launch beachhead is the debut writer**, someone with a finished or nearly finished manuscript, no agent or industry contact, and no way to tell whether anyone is reading their submission. An **equally central group is readers**, especially avid readers who like discovering new voices early and who want to back the authors they love, but have no real way to do so beyond buying a book. Also **equally central are publishers and editors**, who receive manuscripts in inconsistent formats and need a unified way to review them (simulated in the prototype).

What frustrates them today: writers submit by hand, in different formats, with silence in return and a nagging fear of theft. Readers follow authors across scattered social feeds and have no direct line to either authors or publishers. Both feel the literary world is closed, slow, and one-directional.

## 02 — Initial Recruitment Profile (Debut Writer)

**Demographics (assumed):**
- Age: 20s to 40s, with a wide tail
- Gender: no assumption
- Location: Romania at launch; Romanian- and English-speaking literary communities. International audiences are a later expansion.
- Income: varied; writing is usually not their main income
- Life stage: students, early-career professionals, or career-changers writing alongside another job

**Psychographics:**
- **Values:** craft, authenticity, being taken seriously, and creative independence.
- **Aspirations:** to be published, to be read, and to be recognized as a writer.
- **Self-identity:** "I'm a writer" is true to them, but they feel unvalidated until someone in the industry says so.
- **Brands they likely love:** Goodreads, Substack, Notion, independent bookshops (assumed, to verify).
- **Content they likely consume:** writing-advice newsletters, #WritingCommunity posts, publisher and agent blogs, BookTok and Bookstagram.
- **Communities:** online writing groups, workshops, critique circles, NaNoWriMo-style challenges.

**Behavioral patterns (assumed):**
- Research-heavy: they check publisher guidelines carefully, and each submission is stressful and emotionally loaded.
- The trigger is finishing a draft, or getting a rejection or silence after a submission.
- They discover tools through other writers, newsletters and social media.
- They need to trust a tool before uploading unpublished work.

## 03 — Audience Personas (all fictional hypotheses)

### Mara, 27 — Debut novelist and part-time copywriter

*"I finished my novel six months ago. I've sent it to eleven places and I honestly don't know if anyone has even opened it."*

**Their World:** Mara writes at night after work. She keeps her submissions in a spreadsheet, each publisher wants a different package, and she checks her inbox obsessively. She follows other writers online but feels like an outsider to the publishing world.

**Goals:**
- Get her manuscript in front of the right publishers without formatting it eleven times.
- Know where each submission stands.
- Learn whether her opening chapters actually hold a reader.

**Frustrations:**
- Silence after submitting.
- Feedback that is rare, late, or vague.
- Fear that her unpublished text could be copied or leaked.

**What they need from a brand like this:** A safe, clear process that treats her like a professional, with control over access to her work, status she can see, and feedback she can act on.

**Decision triggers:** She'd try it if it promises one-click multi-publisher submission and visible status. She'd hesitate if she doesn't trust the privacy of her manuscript or if no real publishers are on the platform.

**Channels:** Writing communities, newsletters, Bluesky and Instagram writing circles, writing-craft YouTube.

**How Lisette solves it:** Lisette lets Mara submit once, track every response, and share selected excerpts with beta-readers after any required publisher approvals, with her full manuscript in private storage, open only to the publishers she picks.

### Tomás, 34 — Avid reader and beta-reader, works in IT

*"I read fifty books a year and I'd love to help new authors, but I don't know where to find them, and following them across five platforms is a mess."*

**Their World:** Tomás reads on his commute, tracks his books in a notebook or a basic app, and enjoys discovering unknown authors before they are famous. He recommends books to friends constantly.

**Goals:**
- Discover new voices before everyone else.
- Keep one tidy library of what he's read and wants to read.
- Feel that his reading actually helps authors he likes.

**Frustrations:**
- Authors are scattered across social platforms, newsletters and sites.
- Libraries live in apps that feel cold and don't connect to authors.
- His only way to support an author is to buy the book, and he can't help earlier than that.

**What they need from a brand like this:** A warm, simple library, early access to fresh writing, and visible ways to back authors, such as following, reading excerpts early, reviewing, and sharing.

**Decision triggers:** Scanning books by ISBN into a library in seconds, and early excerpts from authors he follows. He'd hesitate if the feed feels empty or the app feels like yet another social network.

**Channels:** Bookstagram and BookTok, reading-community forums, book newsletters, friends' recommendations.

**How Lisette solves it:** Lisette connects Tomás's personal collection to authors and publishers on the platform, relevant literary events and permitted early excerpts. Scanning a book is a starting point for discovery, not just cataloguing or rating.

### Elena, 41 — Acquisitions editor at an independent publisher

*"We get submissions as PDFs, Word files, pasted emails. Half of them don't follow our guidelines, and I can't give every author a proper answer."*

**Their World:** Elena's inbox is full of mismatched submissions. She wants good new voices but has little time to read, and she wants to give authors respectful, structured replies.

**Goals:**
- Receive manuscripts in one consistent format that matches the house guidelines.
- Spot promising voices quickly.
- Give structured feedback without a heavy workload.

**Frustrations:**
- Unstructured, inconsistent submissions.
- No easy way to reply at scale.
- Little visibility into whether a manuscript has real reader interest.

**What they need from a brand like this:** A tidy review workflow and a steady flow of writers who read the guidelines.

**Decision triggers:** Saving time and seeing how readers respond to an author's excerpts, when the author chooses to share it. She'd hesitate if it adds one more tool to learn or if the submitters are low quality.

**Channels:** Industry newsletters, book fairs, publisher networks. (In the prototype this persona is simulated.)

**How Lisette solves it:** Lisette gives Elena a unified submission format, a channel for structured feedback to authors and, when an author shares them, aggregated reading patterns on that author's excerpts. There is no audience score.

## 04 — Audience Language

*All of this is hypothesized and should be validated through real interviews.*

**Their problem:**
- "I have no idea if anyone read my submission."
- "Every publisher wants something different."
- "I'm scared someone will steal my work."
- "I don't know where to find new authors I'd actually like."

**Their desired outcome:**
- "I want to be read."
- "I want honest feedback I can use."
- "I want to know where I stand."
- "I want to help the authors I love get discovered."

**Why they've chosen competitors:**
- Goodreads and StoryGraph: "That's where everyone's books are."
- Submittable and Query Tracker: "It's the only way to keep track of submissions."
- Social media: "That's where the writing community is."

**Brand-building implication:** Lead with relief and clarity, in phrases like "know where your submission stands", "be read", and "help the authors you love get found". Use simple, honest words like "manuscript", "beta-reader", "excerpt", and "your shelf". Avoid hype and tech jargon.

## 05 — Who This Brand Is Not For

- **Established, agented authors**: they already have industry channels and little need for a submission hub (for now).
- **Casual readers who want only a quick ratings app**: Goodreads already serves them, and Lisette's value comes from connection to authors.
- **Anyone looking to pay or be paid for books**: there is no financial layer for now.
- **Self-publishing writers focused on selling directly**: Lisette's route is through publishers, not direct sales.
- **Content hunters seeking mass-market virality**: Lisette is for a literary, craft-focused community.

## 06 — Audience Implications

**Messaging:** Lead with the writer's pain (silence, scattered submissions, fear of theft) and the reader's wish (to help the authors they love). The big idea is one hub where books move from draft to reader and the people who love them back the authors.

**Channels:** Writing communities, BookTok / Bookstagram, writing newsletters, and university creative-writing circles. Start where debut writers and avid readers already gather.

**Voice:** Warm, encouraging, clear. Treat writers as professionals and readers as allies. No hype. (Matches the approved tone in `context.md`.)

**Product and experience:**
- Trust comes first: show clearly that manuscripts are stored privately, and who can see what.
- The network must not feel empty at launch, so use clearly labelled test content for the demo and recruit real beta participants; never present fictional accounts as real community activity.
- Make reader support visible: follow, early-read, review, share, and show authors that it happened. Acknowledge meaningful completed actions gently, without points, rankings, streaks or pressure to return.
- Keep publisher-side workflows simple and unified. Literary events and location discoveries also connect readers with authors and publishers; they are priority product concepts, with release scope still to define.
